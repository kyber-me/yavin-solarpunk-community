# Yavin — Research Objective and Orientation

**Document type** — Foundational research proposal and technical guidance for Yavin.

## 1. Central question

> Is it possible to build a small self-sustaining society based on technology, sustainability, and cooperation?

Yavin investigates the conditions, choices, and constraints that would affect the creation and continued functioning of such a community. Its viability is an open question. The research allows for partial results, contradictions, external dependencies, and the revision or abandonment of hypotheses.

The meanings of “small society” and “self-sustaining” will be specified as each study requires. Size, time horizon, needs met, available resources, and degree of external dependence are all dimensions to define. Exchanges with other communities may be part of the scenarios studied.

## 2. Research problem

Solarpunk offers cultural references and values for imagining desirable futures. Yavin examines how those values relate to material conditions, technical choices, collective organization, and conflicts between needs.

Expanding the energy supply, for example, may raise costs, require maintenance, or increase reliance on external components. Evaluating a technical solution also involves asking who maintains it, how its benefits are distributed, and what happens when it fails.

## 3. Overall objective

Use a progressively developed simulation to investigate the conditions under which a small society oriented toward technology, sustainability, and cooperation could sustain itself. Yavin’s research follows two lines of inquiry:

- **The community under study.** Observe the needs, resources, infrastructure, dependencies, and constraints that affect its ability to sustain itself.
- **The process of inquiry and decision-making.** Observe how human participants and AI agents formulate questions, use references, evaluate alternatives, handle disagreements, and document the reasons for decisions.

Identifying a relevant problem and formulating a more precise question are also research outcomes.

## 4. Starting point and choice of the first research topic

Yavin starts almost from scratch so the research can trace how a need is recognized, what alternatives emerge, how discussion changes a proposal, and what consequences follow a choice. The project starts with a solarpunk orientation, the Project Founder as a participant, and Silas as the first AI collaborator. References, calculations, and tools can support the research, with their sources, assumptions, and limitations made explicit.

Yavin leaves its first research topic open so that a concrete question can emerge from discussion or observed needs. Energy is one possible topic, with generation, storage, and distribution as potential lines of inquiry.

Once a question emerges, the inquiry can begin by making it specific and testable. The scenario, time period, and comparison criteria can be specified as the question requires. Values assumed for a scenario should be identified as hypotheses, with their provenance clearly distinguished from that of observed data.

## 5. Questions for the simulation

- Which needs arise first, how do they evolve, and where are the community’s vulnerabilities concentrated?
- Which decisions recur, which can wait, and how should they be prioritized when attention and resources are limited?
- Under what conditions do technology, sustainability, and cooperation reinforce one another or come into tension?
- Which cutting-edge technologies could contribute to a sustainable community, under what conditions, and how do they compare with established or simpler solutions in terms of cost, maintenance, access, environmental impact, and dependencies?
- How should conversations proceed when participants face disagreement, uncertainty, or limited evidence? Which choices call for new data, experiments, or reconsideration?

Supply, distribution, maintenance, scarcity, failures, and external dependence are examples of situations to investigate. Economics and governance can be explored in greater depth when they help answer a concrete question.

## 6. Simulation, human participation, and multi-agent orchestration

The simulation is the proposed means of tracking problems, hypotheses, conversations, experiments, and decisions over time. Multi-agent orchestration is a development direction for studying how contributions from AI agents can be coordinated. Agreement among agents may stem from shared sources or assumptions. Evaluation should consider the independence of the evidence and make gaps, repetition, disagreements, and coordination difficulties visible.

Alongside the Project Founder’s role in conducting the research, Yavin may explore having people act within the simulation and interact with its environment and AI agents. Their roles, modes of interaction, and how this participation would be implemented remain open.

Yavin uses a hybrid model of initiative. The Project Founder can introduce questions, objectives, and constraints, and the lab can identify problems and propose investigations as it develops. The division of responsibilities, coordination mechanisms, and degree of autonomy will be defined as the research requires.

## 7. Research and decision records

A record of an investigation in Yavin should make it possible to revisit the following in as much detail as the question requires:

- The question, its origin, and the needs, constraints, and assumptions considered.
- Hypotheses, alternatives, disagreements, sources, and gaps in information.
- The analysis or experiment, its conditions and limitations, and the consequences observed.
- The reasons for approved decisions, open or newly identified questions, and reasons to revisit a choice.

The record itself should make hypotheses, evidence, proposals, deliberations, and approved decisions identifiable. A proposal, even when shown on the map, remains a proposal until a decision approves its incorporation into the simulated world. Its viability requires adequate evidence. The format of the record can vary with the investigation.

## 8. Expected outcomes and limits of the simulation

Yavin expects to produce knowledge about the community’s needs, recurring choices, alternatives, dependencies, and critical challenges, and to learn how people and agents conduct an investigation. A refuted hypothesis, an identified limitation, or a justified comparison is a useful outcome.

Conclusions depend on the scenarios, data, and models used. Narratives produced by agents can generate hypotheses that require technical validation. Viability outside the simulation depends on real-world evidence, and conclusions about human cooperation require evidence of human behavior.

Running costs, data availability, continuity of context, tool quality, and model simplifications may limit the research. When interpreting an outcome, identify whether the limitation comes from the community under study, the scenario, or the means used to study it.
