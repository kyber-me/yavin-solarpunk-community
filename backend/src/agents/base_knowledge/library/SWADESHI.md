# Swadeshi

## Definition

*Swadeshi* derives from Sanskrit *sva* (one's own) and *deśa* (country). In the Indian independence movement, it meant supporting Indian production and institutions as a means of building economic self-reliance under British rule. The **Swadeshi movement** commonly refers to the campaign that took shape around the partition of Bengal in 1905. **Gandhian swadeshi** also describes a broader ethic of service to one's neighbours and support for local livelihoods. [Indira Gandhi National Open University, “Swadeshi”](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/33670/1/Unit-15.pdf).

## Historical development

### Economic arguments before 1905

Dadabhai Naoroji argued that the transfer of wealth from India to Britain helped impoverish India. His 1901 book documents an economic critique of colonial rule that preceded the Bengal campaign. [Naoroji, *Poverty and Un-British Rule in India*](https://www.ndl.iitkgp.ac.in/ch_document/internet_archive/ia_books/povertyunbritish00naoruoft).

### The Bengal campaign

On **7 August 1905**, a meeting at [Calcutta Town Hall](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kolkata%20Town%20Hall) adopted a boycott of British goods in protest at the impending [partition of Bengal](<https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Parti%C3%A7%C3%A3o_de_Bengala_(1905)>). The partition took effect on **16 October 1905** under Viceroy George Curzon. The colonial government cited administrative reasons. Opponents saw the division as an attempt to weaken Bengali political organisation. [Banglapedia, “Partition of Bengal, 1905”](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Partition_of_Bengal%2C_1905) · [D. Subramanyam Reddy, *Outlines of Indian History*](https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp01/chapter/ndian-national-movement-1905-1911-vandemataram-and-swadeshi-movements/).

Boycotts and public meetings went alongside efforts to support Indian textile producers and other manufacturers, including match and glass factories. Activists also built educational alternatives, including the [National Council of Education in Bengal](https://jadavpuruniversity.in/about/history/), founded in 1906. The campaign spread beyond Bengal and helped bring [*swaraj*, or self-rule](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Swaraj), into the programme of the [Indian National Congress](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Congresso_Nacional_Indiano). [Banglapedia](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Partition_of_Bengal%2C_1905) · [Reddy, *Outlines of Indian History*](https://ebooks.inflibnet.ac.in/icp01/chapter/ndian-national-movement-1905-1911-vandemataram-and-swadeshi-movements/).

Participation varied by region and community. Some Muslim leaders opposed partition, while others in eastern Bengal supported the new province. Religious symbolism, disputes over boycott tactics and communal tensions shaped the campaign. The British announced the reversal of the partition in December 1911. [Banglapedia on the partition](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Partition_of_Bengal%2C_1905) · [Banglapedia on its reversal](https://en.banglapedia.org/index.php?title=Annulment_of_Bengal_Partition).

### Gandhi, khadi and village industry

Gandhi made hand-spinning and the use of [*khadi*](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Khadi), cloth woven from hand-spun yarn, part of a wider campaign for self-rule. Spinning gave households a way to participate, and buying khadi supported spinners and weavers. Gandhi applied the same principle to other village industries and to the institutions people could help improve in their own communities. [Indira Gandhi National Open University](https://egyankosh.ac.in/bitstream/123456789/33670/1/Unit-15.pdf).

In his writings, Gandhi gave priority to producers and neighbours whose livelihoods were affected by what people bought. He accepted imports that met a genuine need without undermining those livelihoods. He cited surgical instruments as one example. [Gandhi's writings on swadeshi, collected by Mani Bhavan Gandhi Sangrahalaya](https://www.gandhi-manibhavan.org/gandhian-philosophy/philosophy-swadeshi-meaning.html).

## Cultural legacy and later uses

- **Literature.** Rabindranath Tagore's *The Home and the World* depicts disagreements over nationalist mobilisation and the use of coercion to enforce a boycott. [English translation at Project Gutenberg](https://www.gutenberg.org/cache/epub/7166/pg7166-images.html).
- **Handloom heritage.** India first observed National Handloom Day on 7 August 2015. The date commemorates the 1905 campaign and recognises the role of handloom workers. [Government of India, Press Information Bureau](https://www.pib.gov.in/newsite/PrintRelease.aspx?lang=2&reg=48&relid=192496) · [The Indian Express explainer cited by Wikipedia](https://indianexpress.com/article/explained/why-is-august-7-called-national-handloom-day-6544586/).
- **Industrial policy.** Later appeals to swadeshi have invoked domestic manufacturing and economic self-reliance in different policy settings, including [Make in India](https://pt.wikipedia.org/wiki/Make_in_India) and [Atmanirbhar Bharat](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Atmanirbhar%20Bharat). [Vikas Pathak, *The Indian Express*](https://indianexpress.com/article/political-pulse/gandhis-khadi-to-modis-no-matter-the-colour-of-investment-a-new-shade-of-swadeshi-10214836/).

## Related concepts in Yavin

- **[Jugaad](Jugaad.md).** Jugaad describes how people improvise with available materials and skills. Swadeshi asks how purchasing and production can sustain the people and institutions that supply those resources.
- **Low-tech.** The charkha offers a case for studying how a tool, a learnable skill and a market for the output can support distributed production.
- **Solarpunk.** The historical case gives Yavin a question about community infrastructure. Which capabilities can residents build and sustain locally, and which depend on cooperation with other communities? [Yavin founding minutes in Portuguese](../atas/ATA_001_Fundacao_de_Yavin.md).
